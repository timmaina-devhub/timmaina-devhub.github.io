My Story

Background

How I got here

Early curiosity

Back in high school I was the person who enjoyed working in Excel more than most people enjoyed anything. When I discovered data science, it felt like Excel had leveled up and taken over the rest of my life. I didn't come in obsessed with statistics or machine learning theory — I came in because the tools let me keep doing what I already liked.

Real work first

I like building things that run. The part that stuck wasn't the report aesthetic — it was training a model, watching it learn, and using it to help someone make a better decision. Fraud detection. Demand forecasting. Churn prevention. That's the work I keep showing up for.

Leadership

I revived an inactive student club and led it for two years. The specific wins are in the section below, but what mattered more was what it taught me: how to communicate clearly, delegate without micromanaging, and stay accountable when things went sideways.

Community

Outside school, I've helped run my family's business, supported friends and younger students when they needed it, and looked for small ways to give back. Details are in the section below — the throughline is that I show up when something needs doing.

What I value outside work

I'm a football fan, play video games, and watch cyber/crime/privacy shows. I'm straightforward about goals, easy to collaborate with, and honest about what I don't know yet. If you're looking for someone who keeps promises and stays calm under pressure, I'm a safe bet.

What I'd do differently

If I were starting today I'd go straight to real organisational data instead of synthesising anything. I'd also interview the people using the outputs before writing modelling code — that context changes which features matter and which visualisations are actually worth building.
